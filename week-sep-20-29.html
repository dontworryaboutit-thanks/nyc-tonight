Ten days, Sep 20–29.

Sunday through the 29th, filtered hard. Everything below comes out of the dashboard's own scrape — club calendars, Resident Advisor, Metrograph, Songkick — then re-ranked by hand against the taste profile rather than by the scorer, which keeps rewarding the word “Mike.”

Two things make this window unusually good. John Coltrane turns 100 on Wednesday the 23rd, and Dizzy's is running a six-night centennial. And there's an accidental Ryuichi Sakamoto weekend — a piano program in an Episcopal church on the 25th, then both Sakamoto documentaries at Metrograph on the 26th and 27th.

Built from the last clean scrape (Sep 16). Metrograph's calendar only runs through the 27th, so the 28th–29th have no film listings yet. Village Vanguard, Roulette, The Stone, Issue Project Room, Film Forum and Anthology aren't in the feed with usable dates — worth a manual look before you lock a night.

If you only do five things

  1. A Winged Victory for the Sullen — Pioneer Works, Mon 28 & Tue 29. Stars of the Lid's Adam Wiltzie and composer Dustin O'Halloran, strings and tape hiss, in a converted Red Hook ironworks. Pan•American opens. Two nights, so it's the easiest thing here to schedule.
  2. Coltrane Centennial — Dizzy's Club, Tue 22 – Sun 27. Six different bands over six nights. The 23rd is the actual hundredth birthday.
  3. The Sakamoto run — St. Bartholomew's Fri 25, Metrograph Sat 26 & Sun 27. Piano program, then Diaries, then Opus. Doing all three is the best three-day arc in this window.
  4. Alabaster DePlume — The Rooftop at Elsewhere, Tue 22. Spiritual-jazz saxophone and spoken word, outdoors, on a roof, probably the last warm week for it.
  5. Yi Yi — Metrograph, Sun 20, 5:30pm. Edward Yang on DCP, three hours, and the retrospective continues with A Brighter Summer Day on Monday.

A film day with a dub tail. Two Taiwanese masters at Metrograph, and two separate parties built around echo and sound-system listening.

Edward Yang, 2000, 173 min, DCP. A family in Taipei seen from three generations at once — the one film that would justify the whole week on its own. Part of a Yang retrospective that runs into next week.
